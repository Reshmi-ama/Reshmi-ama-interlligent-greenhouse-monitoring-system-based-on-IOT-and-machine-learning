Week 3: VLC Downlink System Design

Designing the Visible Light Communication (VLC) downlink architecture and finalizing the hardware components required for serial data transmission.

Major Outcome — VLC Architecture & Hardware Selected

We successfully mapped out the foundational architecture for the Visible Light Communication (VLC) downlink system. All necessary hardware components for the transmitter (LED driver side) and receiver (photodiode side) have been identified and documented for procurement.

What We Did This Week

  • Initiated Phase 2: System Design, focusing heavily on the VLC downlink required to transmit environmental control data to the end nodes.
  • Researched serial data transmission mechanisms using microcontrollers (such as Arduino and MSP430) over a visible light medium.
  • Designed the Transmitter Block: Identified the necessity of USB to UART modules, transistor-based driving circuits (e.g., 2N2222 or 2N401), and high-power LEDs capable of rapid modulation.
  • Designed the Receiver Block: Selected photodiode receivers (e.g., MRD500) paired with transimpedance or inverting amplifiers (e.g., LM393/LM358) and voltage comparators to reconstruct the weak optical signals.
  • Analyzed how to convert light signals back into reliable electrical TTL logic levels that the receiving microcontroller's UART pins can interpret.
  • Reviewed practical reference models for VLC hardware setups to better understand real-world limitations, such as ambient light interference and line-of-sight constraints.

Identified Hardware Components

Module Component Purpose in System
Transmitter Microcontroller (e.g., Arduino) Generates and outputs the serial data payload to be transmitted.
Transmitter LED Driver / Switching Transistor Acts as a high-speed switch to modulate the LED based on the UART digital signal.
Transmitter High-Power LED Converts the modulated electrical signal into rapid visible light variations.
Receiver Photodiode (e.g., MRD500) Detects the variations in visible light and converts them back into a micro-current.
Receiver Transimpedance / Inverting Amp Amplifies the extremely weak photodiode current into a usable, measurable voltage level.
Receiver Voltage Comparator Squares the amplified analog signal back into clean digital logic (0s and 1s) for the receiver board.
