// Design Overview

VLC Downlink Architecture

In our system architecture, the downlink channel carries control commands from the central server down to the cultivation environment — including actuator signals for lighting schedules, humidity control, and ventilation triggers. We chose Visible Light Communication (VLC) for the downlink because the mushroom houses already require artificial lighting, allowing us to embed data into the light signal without adding separate RF infrastructure.

This week we mapped out the complete signal path: from the microcontroller generating the serial data, through the LED driver and high-brightness LED, across the optical channel inside the mushroom house, to the photodiode receiver and signal conditioning circuit that recovers the data.

Identified Components

Based on our research from reference implementations and project reviews this week, we identified the following components as necessary for the VLC downlink circuit.

TX — Transmitter
Arduino Uno / Nano

Microcontroller for generating serial data and controlling LED modulation via a digital output pin.

ATmega328P 5V logic UART serial
TX — Transmitter
High-Power LED

White or IR LED used as the optical transmitter. Modulated at high frequency to encode data in the light beam.

High brightness Fast switching OOK compatible
TX — Transmitter
NPN Transistor (BC547 / 2N2222)

Switching transistor to drive the LED at required current levels from the Arduino's digital pin output.

BC547 / 2N2222 LED driver Fast switching
TX — Transmitter
Current Limiting Resistor

Resistor to protect LED and transistor from excess current. Value calculated based on LED forward voltage and supply.

220Ω – 470Ω LED protection
RX — Receiver
Photodiode / Phototransistor

Optical receiver component that converts modulated light into electrical signal. BPW34 photodiode or L14F1 phototransistor are candidate parts.

BPW34 / L14F1 High sensitivity Fast response
RX — Receiver
Op-Amp (LM358 / LM741)

Amplifies the weak photocurrent signal to a level readable by the Arduino's digital input. Configured as a transimpedance amplifier.

LM358 Signal amplifier Trans-Z amp

Arduino Uno (Receiver)

// Key Findings

Insights from Reference Research

Reviewing the two reference implementations this week gave us practical validation for our component choices. The first reference demonstrated a complete Arduino-based VLC serial data link using OOK modulation, confirming that our microcontroller selection and LED driver approach is feasible at the baud rates we require for sending cultivation commands.

The second reference — a final year engineering project — showed a full Li-Fi system using very similar hardware, validating that the photodiode + op-amp amplifier chain is sufficient for indoor line-of-sight distances typical inside a mushroom house. Both references also used breadboard prototyping first before finalising circuits, which aligns with our planned build sequence.

Next steps will involve procuring the identified components and building the initial transmitter circuit on the breadboard for bench testing.
