// Overview

Research Focus This Week

This week we shifted focus from communication hardware to the agricultural side of the system — specifically, what wavelengths of light actually benefit oyster mushroom cultivation and at what duty cycles. Since our VLC downlink uses visible LEDs as the data carrier, the LED choice has a dual role: communication signal and biological stimulation. Getting the wavelength right means we can serve both purposes simultaneously.

We reviewed published studies on the effects of LED colour on white oyster mushroom yield and physical characteristics, and used these findings to determine the photoperiod settings we will test in our system. We also designed a multi-wavelength testing setup to experimentally validate the configurations before integrating them into the final build.

// Wavelength Research

Optimal Wavelengths for Growth

Based on literature, four wavelength ranges were evaluated for their effect on total fresh weight, cap diameter, stem thickness, and time-to-pinhead emergence.

Blue LED ~475 nm

Most effective wavelength overall. Yields the highest total fresh weight, largest mushroom caps (pileus diameter), and thickest stems. Strongly recommended as the primary light source.

★ Best for yield & size
Green LED 515–530 nm

Highly effective for accelerating the reproductive stage — significantly shortens the time for pinheads to emerge and fruiting bodies to form. Good candidate for early fruiting stimulation.

✓ Best for fast pinning
White LED Broad spectrum

Broad spectrum that accelerates pinhead emergence and supports strong overall yields. Practical choice as it doubles as room illumination and is widely available. Selected for VLC integration.

◎ Practical & versatile
Red LED ~660 nm

Should be avoided during the fruiting phase — suppresses growth, reduces total yield, and causes elongated lower-quality stems. May temporarily promote early mycelium growth but not recommended for general use.

✕ Avoid — reduces yield
⚠

Stage-specific requirement: LED light should only be applied during the reproductive stage (when fruiting bodies are forming). Applying light during the vegetative/mycelium colonisation phase is not recommended and may cause adverse effects.

// On/Off Cycles

Recommended Photoperiods

Three photoperiod configurations were identified from the literature. Each is optimised for a different cultivation goal and will be tested independently in the experimental setup.

Max Yield
3h ON / 21h OFF

Highest fruiting body count

Produces the highest total fresh weight and greatest number of individual fruiting bodies per flush. Best for quantity-focused cultivation.

Max Size
15h ON / 9h OFF

Largest cap & stem diameter

Produces commercial-grade mushrooms with the largest cap diameters and thickest stems. Best for size and appearance quality.

Baseline
12h ON / 12h OFF

Standard control cycle

Standard 12/12 cycle providing stable, consistent results. Used as the control baseline for comparing the other two configurations during testing.

// Experimental Design

Multi-Wavelength Testing Setup

To empirically validate the wavelength and photoperiod findings before integrating them into the full automated system, we designed the following comparative test setup. Three isolated mushroom cultivation boxes will run simultaneously under different LED configurations, monitored by the Arduino-based control system.

// Test Configuration

Test Units

Unit A: Blue LED (~475 nm) — 3h ON / 21h OFF

Unit B: White LED — 15h ON / 9h OFF

Unit C (Control): White LED — 12h ON / 12h OFF

Measurement Metrics

Total fresh weight per flush (grams)

Cap (pileus) diameter (mm)

Stem (stipe) thickness (mm)

Days to pinhead emergence

Control Variables

Temperature: 24–28°C

Humidity: 80–90% RH

Substrate: identical substrate blocks

Strain: white oyster (Pleurotus ostreatus)

Hardware Used

Arduino Uno — timer & LED control

Blue, white high-brightness LEDs

DHT22 — temperature & humidity sensor

NPN transistor driver circuits (per week 3)

Circuit note: The LED driver circuit designed in Week 3 (Arduino → NPN transistor → LED) will be used for all test units. The Arduino's digital output timer controls the on/off cycling. A circuit diagram has been prepared and will be included as a supplementary document.
