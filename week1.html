Week 1: Literature Review

Studying existing IoT greenhouse systems, VLC communication, LoRa protocols, and mushroom cultivation science to identify our research gap.

Completed
Feb – Mar 2026 Week 1 of 14 20+ Papers Reviewed EEE · University of Peradeniya

What We Did This Week

  • Reviewed existing IoT-based greenhouse automation systems and identified that most focus on vegetable crops — very few specifically target mushroom cultivation in Sri Lankan conditions.
  • Studied Visible Light Communication (VLC) fundamentals: modulation techniques (OOK, PPM), LED driver circuits, and photodetector receiver designs for indoor communication.
  • Researched LoRa (Long Range) wireless protocol: coverage range, power consumption, packet size limitations, and suitability for rural agricultural environments in Sri Lanka.
  • Studied mushroom cultivation science: optimal temperature (20–30°C for Oyster), humidity (80–95%), CO₂ levels, light requirements, and the six-stage life cycle from spore to harvest.
  • Investigated CNN-based plant disease detection systems and NoIR imaging techniques using infrared cameras for pre-visual disease identification.
  • Reviewed Fuzzy Logic control systems for agricultural automation — identifying it as suitable for non-linear control of misting and ventilation in mushroom sheds.
  • Identified the research gap: no existing system uses a hybrid VLC downlink + LoRa uplink architecture specifically for mushroom greenhouse automation in Sri Lanka.
  • Created the 14-week project timeline and assigned responsibilities among team members.

Key Findings from Literature

Finding 1 — VLC is ideal for greenhouse downlink

Unlike RF-based systems, VLC is completely immune to electromagnetic interference from motors, fans, and water pumps commonly found in mushroom sheds. Existing LED lighting can be reused as the communication medium, reducing hardware cost.

Finding 2 — LoRa outperforms Wi-Fi for rural sensor uplink

Wi-Fi has limited range (~50m indoors) and fails to penetrate thick mud/wood walls typical of Sri Lankan mushroom sheds. LoRa achieves 2–15km range, consumes <50mA active current, and a single gateway can cover an entire village of farms.

Finding 3 — NoIR 940nm imaging detects disease before visible symptoms

Near-Infrared Reflectance (NIR) drops in diseased mushroom tissue before colour changes appear to the human eye. The 940nm wavelength is completely invisible — ensuring zero disruption to the mushroom's biological dark cycle during monitoring.

Finding 4 — Mushroom farming in Sri Lanka is mostly manual

Sri Lankan mushroom farmers (mainly Oyster and Milky varieties) spend 3–4 hours daily on manual spraying and shed monitoring. Crop failure rate is high due to inconsistent humidity and temperature control — directly addressable by our system.
