Week 6: Hardware Circuit Design

Designed and built a hardware circuit to test mushroom growth stimulation using LEDs — including an ESP32 microcontroller, IRLZ44N MOSFETs, and a dual-channel LED driver for Blue (450nm) and Green command LEDs on a 12V supply.

Completed
May 2026 Week 6 of 14 Circuit Design EEE · University of Peradeniya

What We Did This Week

  • Designed a hardware circuit on a breadboard to simulate and test mushroom growth stimulation using LED lighting controlled by an ESP32 microcontroller.
  • Selected the IRLZ44N N-channel MOSFET as the switching element for both LED channels — chosen for its logic-level gate drive compatibility with ESP32 (3.3V GPIO) and its ability to handle 12V LED loads.
  • Designed a dual-channel LED driver circuit — Channel A for the Blue LED strip (450nm, mushroom growth stimulation) and Channel B for the Green command signal LED, both controlled independently via GPIO 26 and GPIO 27.
  • Added 10kΩ gate resistors to limit inrush current and protect ESP32 GPIO pins from back-EMF during MOSFET switching.
  • Added 100kΩ pull-down resistors to GND on each gate to ensure MOSFETs remain fully OFF when GPIO pins are in high-impedance state — preventing accidental LED activation during boot.
  • Used a single 12V DC adapter as the power supply — 12V feeds both LED channels via the MOSFETs, while the ESP32 is powered via its VIN pin (which accepts 7–12V).
  • Established a shared GND between the 12V supply and the ESP32 to ensure proper MOSFET switching reference.
  • Tested the circuit to verify independent PWM control of both LED channels from the ESP32 — confirming that the Blue and Green LEDs respond correctly to GPIO 26 and GPIO 27 signals.

📐 Circuit Overview

The 12V DC adapter powers both the ESP32 (via VIN) and the LED channels. GPIO 26 drives MOSFET A (Blue LED — Chamber A, 450nm) and GPIO 27 drives MOSFET B (Green LED — Command Signal). Gate resistors (10kΩ) limit switching current; 100kΩ pull-downs ensure safe default-OFF state.

Key Design Decisions

⚡ Why IRLZ44N MOSFET?

The IRLZ44N is a logic-level MOSFET — it fully turns ON with just 3.3V on the gate, which is exactly what the ESP32 GPIO outputs. Standard MOSFETs require 10V+ gate drive and would not switch properly from ESP32 GPIO pins directly.

🔒 Why 100kΩ Pull-down Resistors?

During ESP32 boot-up, GPIO pins float to undefined states before the firmware takes control. Without pull-down resistors, the MOSFETs could randomly turn ON — causing LEDs to flash unexpectedly. The 100kΩ pull-downs hold the gate LOW (OFF) by default.

🔗 Why Single 12V Supply for Both ESP32 and LEDs?

The ESP32 VIN pin accepts up to 12V and has an onboard regulator — so a single 12V adapter can power both the ESP32 and the 12V LED channels simultaneously, simplifying the power design and reducing component count.

💡 Why GPIO 26 for Blue and GPIO 27 for Green?

GPIO 26 and 27 are DAC-capable pins on the ESP32 and support PWM output — allowing precise dimming and modulation of both LED channels independently. This is essential for future VLC signal encoding via the Blue LED channel.
