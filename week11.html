🎯 Major Milestone — First Live Deployment

Our hardware is now physically installed and running inside a real commercial mushroom growing house. The Raspberry Pi camera is capturing images every 30 minutes, uploading them to Google Drive — building the dataset for our ML model.

What We Did This Week

  • Visited Uhumeeya Global Green Agro Pvt Ltd and successfully installed the Raspberry Pi camera circuit inside the mushroom growing house.
  • Mounted the camera to capture clear images of mushroom substrate bags at multiple growth stages on the metal racks.
  • Configured the Raspberry Pi to automatically capture a photo every 30 minutes using a scheduled cron job — no manual triggering needed.
  • Connected the Raspberry Pi to the farm's network and set up automatic upload to Google Drive — images are accessible for download from anywhere.
  • Verified the full pipeline: camera captures → image saved locally → uploaded to Google Drive → downloadable by the team.
  • Took reference photos of the growing rooms — both the main oyster mushroom room and the second room — documenting the real environment our system will operate in.
  • This image data will be used to build our own mushroom growth dataset for training the CNN-based disease detection and growth stage classification model.

⏱️ Capture Frequency — Every 30 Minutes

48 images per day per camera — enough to track growth stage transitions, detect early signs of disease, and build a time-series dataset showing full mushroom development from pinning to harvest.

☁️ Google Drive Auto-Upload

Images are automatically uploaded to a shared Google Drive folder — the team can monitor the growing room remotely and download new images daily for dataset labelling and CNN model training.

🗄️ Building Our Own Dataset

Since no Sri Lankan mushroom cultivation dataset exists publicly, this camera system will generate our own labelled dataset of Oyster mushroom growth stages under local conditions — directly improving our ML model's accuracy for Sri Lankan farms.
