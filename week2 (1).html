What We Did This Week

  • Visited Uhumeeya Global Green Agro Pvt Ltd and conducted structured discussions with the farm owner and cultivators about the full mushroom growing process.
  • Collected detailed data on environmental parameters — the farm currently monitors temperature, humidity, and CO₂ manually, four times per day (at 8am, 12pm, 4pm, and occasionally a 5th reading).
  • Inspected 5 Button mushroom rooms and 4 Oyster mushroom rooms — all indoor, climate-controlled growing chambers with metal racks and LED lighting.
  • Learned that humidity is controlled by putting water on the floor — no misting or drip systems — and a wet bulb/dry bulb method is used to check relative humidity.
  • Discovered that fan speed and ventilation are controlled manually — no automated airflow management is currently in place.
  • Identified that CO₂ and temperature are the most critical parameters — buildup of CO₂ to high levels directly damages yield, and temperature swings are managed using ACs in button mushroom rooms and cooling pads in oyster rooms.
  • Collected observations on infrastructure: metal racks present (signal consideration for LoRa/VLC), LED lighting installed, electrical power sources identified.
  • Farm owner confirmed they prefer heavy automation — manual measuring and controlling is difficult, especially with Sri Lankan weather variability.
  • Secured agreement from the farm owner to use the farmhouse as the live testing environment for our final system.

Farm Interview — Questions & Answers

1. General Farm Information & Scale
What type of mushrooms do you grow?
Button and Oyster mushrooms.
What is the size of the farm?
About 5 rooms for Button mushrooms and 4 rooms for Oyster mushrooms — all indoor growing chambers.
How many growing cycles do you have?
Oyster mushrooms take about 30 days to reach the first fruiting cycle. Button mushrooms reach it at about 18 days.
Is the farm indoor or semi-outdoor?
Fully indoor. All growing chambers are enclosed.
2. Environmental Parameters Currently Monitored
What parameters do you currently monitor manually?
Temperature, Humidity, and CO₂ — all monitored manually using a CO₂ meter and wet bulb/dry bulb method for relative humidity.
How often do you take readings?
4 times a day — at 8am, 12pm, 4pm, and sometimes a 5th reading. This is a significant daily time commitment for farmers.
Have you faced crop loss due to environmental fluctuations?
Yes — CO₂ and temperature are the most critical factors. Uncontrolled fluctuations affect many other aspects of mushroom growth and lead to direct yield loss.
3. Challenges & Pain Points
What is the most difficult part of maintaining the environment manually?
CO₂ control and Relative Humidity (RH) are the hardest to manage manually. They require constant attention.
Issues with high/low humidity?
With very high humidity (close to 100%), mushrooms do not grow well. The balance must be carefully maintained.
Issues with CO₂ buildup?
CO₂ buildup to high levels directly damages the yield. This is one of the most serious problems identified during the visit.
Issues with temperature swings?
Temperature is managed with ACs in button mushroom rooms and cooling pads in oyster mushroom rooms. With AC, severe temperature swings are less common.
4. Ventilation & Airflow
How do you currently control ventilation?
Using fans — but fan speed and other settings are controlled manually. No automated airflow system is currently in place.
Do you monitor air circulation inside the growing room?
No. Air circulation inside chambers is not currently monitored.
Have you noticed differences near vents vs. corners?
Yes — at the level of the AC output, bags tend to dry out more easily, which affects growth in those areas.
5. Irrigation & Humidity Control
How do you water the mushrooms?
Water is put on the floor — misting and spraying are not used at this farm. Floor water evaporates to raise ambient humidity.
Do you use any timers or sensors for humidity?
The wet bulb / dry bulb method is used to check relative humidity. No automated humidity sensors or timers are in use.
6. Actuators & Manual Tasks
What actions do you take when parameters go out of range?
Fans are turned on manually. Windows may be opened. Actions depend entirely on manual observation — there is no automated response system.
Would you prefer automation for any of these tasks?
Yes — they prefer automation heavily. Manual measuring and controlling is not easy, especially given changing weather conditions in Sri Lanka.
7. Farm Layout & Infrastructure
What type of lighting is installed?
LED lighting is installed in the growing rooms — this is compatible with our planned VLC downlink system which uses LEDs as the communication medium.
Are there any metal racks or structures that could block signals?
Metal racks are present in the growing rooms. This is an important consideration for LoRa signal penetration and VLC line-of-sight placement in our system design.
8. Safety & Reliability
What would happen if a fan stayed ON accidentally for too long?
If fans run for too long, a low pressure condition builds up in the room, causing insects to enter and harm the crop. This means our system must include safeguards against over-ventilation.
What level of reliability would you expect from an automated system?
High reliability is expected — the farm cannot afford system failures that could lead to crop damage or loss.
9. Farm Records & Data
Do you keep any logs of environmental conditions or yield?
Yes — the farm keeps records of their environmental conditions. This historical data will be valuable for training our ML models and validating our system's performance benchmarks.

Key Design Insights from Visit

💨 CO₂ Control is the #1 Priority

CO₂ and RH were identified as the hardest parameters to control. High CO₂ directly damages yield. Our automated LoRa-connected CO₂ sensors + fan control system directly addresses this critical need.

🌡️ Separate Climate Systems per Mushroom Type

Button mushroom rooms use ACs while oyster rooms use cooling pads. Our system must handle both room types with configurable control profiles per species.

📡 Metal Racks — Signal Planning Required

Metal racks in all rooms affect radio signal propagation. LoRa gateway and VLC LED placement must account for these physical obstructions in the final system design.

⚠️ Fan Safety — Must Include Limits

Extended fan operation causes low pressure buildup that lets insects in. Our automated control system must include maximum run-time limits and pressure-aware logic for fan actuation.

✅ LED Lighting Already Installed

LED lights are already in place in all growing rooms — this means our VLC downlink can integrate with the existing lighting infrastructure without major additional hardware installation.
