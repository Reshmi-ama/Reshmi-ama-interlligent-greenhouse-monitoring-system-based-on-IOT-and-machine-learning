Week 13: ML for Mushroom Stage Detection

Studied machine learning approaches for identifying mushroom growth stages — focusing on detecting pinning and fruiting stages using YOLOv8, while building a custom dataset from farm photos.

📊 Dataset Building In Progress

Photos captured every 30 minutes by the Raspberry Pi camera at Uhumeeya farm are being downloaded, organised, and labelled to build a custom Sri Lankan oyster mushroom dataset for YOLOv8 training.

What We Did This Week

  • Studied machine learning methods for mushroom growth stage classification — focusing on computer vision approaches suited to detecting visual changes in mushroom bags on racks.
  • Identified the two most critical stages to detect: Pinning Stage (pinhead formation) and Fruiting Stage (mature mushroom body development) — both require different LED lighting and environmental conditions.
  • Selected YOLOv8 (You Only Look Once v8) by Ultralytics as the detection model — chosen for its speed, accuracy, and ability to run inference in real-time on edge devices like Raspberry Pi.
  • Reviewed YOLOv8 architecture and training workflow — understanding how to prepare a custom dataset with bounding box annotations for mushroom stage detection.
  • Started downloading and organising photos from Google Drive — images captured by the Raspberry Pi at the Uhumeeya mushroom farm every 30 minutes.
  • Began planning the annotation process — labelling pinning and fruiting stages in each image using bounding boxes to create training data for YOLOv8.
  • Identified that accurate stage detection will allow the system to automatically switch LED colours (Green → Blue) at the right moment without relying on fixed day-count timers.

Why Stage Detection Matters

🟡
Pinning Stage Detection
Tiny pinheads (primordia) start appearing on the surface of substrate bags. The system must detect this to switch from Green LED to Blue LED automatically.
Small white dots Day ~22–25 Switch to Blue LED
🔵
Fruiting Stage Detection
Full mushroom bodies with visible caps and stems. System must detect when caps start curling upward to trigger harvest alert and stop LED cycle.
Visible caps Day ~26–31 Harvest alert
💡 Why Not Fixed Day-Count Timers?

Growth speed varies with temperature, humidity, and CO₂. Fixed timers like "switch to Blue on Day 22" may be too early or too late. Vision-based detection responds to actual biological state, not calendar days — making the system truly intelligent.

Why YOLOv8?

YOLOv8 — You Only Look Once v8

YOLOv8 by Ultralytics is a real-time object detection model that processes the entire image in a single forward pass — making it fast enough for periodic inference on a Raspberry Pi or edge server. It can detect multiple objects (pinheads, fruiting bodies) in one image simultaneously.
