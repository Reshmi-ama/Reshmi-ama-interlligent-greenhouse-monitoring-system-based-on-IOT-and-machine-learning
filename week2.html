Major Outcome — Testing Agreement Secured

The owner of Uhumeeya Global Green Agro Pvt Ltd has agreed to provide their farmhouse as the testing site for our final system. This gives us a real-world mushroom cultivation environment to validate the complete IoT + VLC + LoRa system under actual farming conditions.

What We Did This Week

  • Visited Uhumeeya Global Green Agro Pvt Ltd mushroom farm and met with the farm owner and cultivators to discuss the full mushroom growing process from substrate preparation to harvest.
  • Conducted structured discussions with farmers about the step-by-step procedure of growing Oyster and Button mushrooms — including substrate selection, pasteurisation, inoculation, spawn run, and fruiting stages.
  • Collected detailed information on environmental conditions required for Oyster and Button mushroom cultivation — temperature ranges, humidity levels, CO₂ tolerance, light requirements, and ventilation needs.
  • Discussed the major difficulties farmers face: maintaining consistent humidity manually, detecting diseases early, managing CO₂ levels in closed sheds, and the high daily labour time spent on environmental monitoring.
  • Entered and inspected the closed mushroom growing houses — observed the layout, shelf arrangement, misting systems, ventilation setup, and existing (manual) monitoring methods.
  • Collected photographs of the growing houses, mushroom bags at different growth stages, substrate preparation area, and existing equipment — for use in system design and documentation.
  • Identified that farmers spend 3–4 hours daily on manual water spraying and environmental checks — confirming the core problem our automated system will solve.
  • Secured agreement from the farm owner to use the farmhouse as a live testing environment for our final integrated system in Week 13–14.

Key Difficulties Identified from Farmers

🕐 High Manual Labour Time

Farmers spend 3–4 hours every day manually spraying water to maintain humidity and checking each shed by hand. This is time-consuming and inconsistent — especially at night when no one monitors the sheds.

🌡️ Inconsistent Environmental Control

Temperature and humidity fluctuate significantly during Sri Lankan weather changes (monsoon vs. dry season). Manual adjustments are too slow to respond to sudden changes, leading to crop failure or reduced yield.

🦠 Late Disease Detection

Diseases like Trichoderma (green mold) and Bacterial Blotch are usually spotted only after significant spread — by which time an entire batch may be lost. There is no early warning system currently in use.

💨 CO₂ Buildup in Closed Sheds

In fully enclosed mushroom houses, CO₂ accumulates quickly from mushroom respiration. Without automated ventilation, farmers must manually open vents — often incorrectly timed, causing deformed mushrooms with long thin stems.

📶 No Remote Monitoring

All monitoring is done physically on-site. Farmers cannot check their shed conditions remotely, meaning they must be present at all times or risk undetected problems overnight or during weekends.
